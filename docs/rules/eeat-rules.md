- Precedence: every E-E-A-T rule must follow seo-rules.md. If a rule here ever conflicts with seo-rules.md, seo-rules.md wins and the E-E-A-T rule must be adjusted, never the other way around
- Honesty first: never invent experience, test results, numbers, case studies, testimonials, credentials, or author profiles. Only use facts that really exist in the project data, the database, or material supplied by the owner. If proof is missing, ask for it instead of making it up
- Experience: show first-hand work with specifics only someone who did it would know, such as setup steps, what went wrong, timelines, and measurements. Use honest first-person wording ("we") only for things the team really did
- Original data: include real numbers from the business's own work, such as package prices, delivery times, and completed project counts. Put key numbers in a table, and link the source of every third-party statistic
- Real examples: every in-depth article should contain at least one real example with an outcome. Skip the section if no real example is available, instead of writing a fictional one
- Original images: prefer own photos, screenshots, charts, or recordings over stock imagery. Wrap them in a figure with a descriptive figcaption, and give each image a unique, descriptive alt text (the article title alone is only a fallback). In article markdown, put the caption in the image title (![alt](url "caption")) so it renders as a figure with a figcaption. Label illustrations as "Ilustrasi" and never present an illustration as a real photo or a real result
- Expertise: show who wrote the content and why they are qualified. Use the real team roles, years of experience, and completed projects already published on the site
- Author byline: every article shows a visible author byline that links to the about page with rel="author"
- Author box: every article has an author box labeled "Tentang penulis" (id="author-bio") containing a 2 to 3 sentence bio, the real background of the team, and links to the about page and the contact page. On desktop it sits in a sticky sidebar, and on mobile it sits below the article
- Author bio content: state the role, the years of experience, the completed project count, and why the author is qualified to write about the topic, using only facts that already exist in the project data (stats and team data)
- Author credentials: show the real name and job title of every team member in the author box. Never claim degrees, certifications, or licences that are not documented in the project data
- Person schema: add Person schema with jobTitle and worksFor for real team members, and fill sameAs only with real public profile URLs stored in the optional profiles field of the team data (LinkedIn and similar). Never create fake profiles to satisfy a checker, and keep the schema empty of sameAs until real profiles are supplied
- Topic depth: cover the follow-up questions a reader would ask next through question-style headings and the FAQ block, but never add padding or exceed the word count limit in seo-rules.md just to look thorough
- Authoritativeness: back claims with primary and official sources (official documentation, government, standards bodies, original research) and link the exact page used. Follow the outbound link count in seo-rules.md
- Organization identity: keep Organization schema with logo and sameAs profiles, Article schema with author, datePublished and dateModified, and BreadcrumbList on every article page
- Internal linking: connect related articles and service pages with descriptive anchor text, following the internal link count in seo-rules.md. Every link must have readable anchor text, and image-only links such as the logo need a visually hidden text label
- Trust, security: the production site must be served over HTTPS only. Localhost warnings about HTTP or a canonical pointing to the production domain are expected in development and are not defects
- Trust, transparency: keep the about page and the contact page linked in the footer on every page
- Trust, legal links: every page shows a footer legal row (nav labeled "Informasi hukum") with the privacy policy, terms and conditions, editorial policy, and contact page. Keep the labels in Indonesian and the routes /privasi, /syarat-ketentuan, /kebijakan-editorial, and /kontak
- Trust, legal pages: the privacy, terms, and editorial pages share the LegalDocument layout, keep their sections in the legal_pages table (or the content pack in demo mode), and show a visible "Diperbarui terakhir" date. They must be listed in the sitemap
- Trust, legal content: write legal and policy text only from facts that exist in the project data (packages, services, contact details, stats) and generic commitments the team really keeps. Never invent payment terms, refund terms, guarantees, or review processes. The owner reviews every legal page before it goes live
- Trust, editorial policy: the editorial policy covers purpose, authorship, sources, honest use of experience and data, dates and updates, corrections, and the commercial interest of the site. Add a statement about AI use only when the owner confirms the real practice
- Trust, author link: every author box links to the editorial policy page
- Trust, machine-readable signals: mark the privacy link with rel="privacy-policy" and the terms link with rel="terms-of-service", keep a Contact link in the main header navigation as well as the footer, and keep ContactPoint and publishingPrinciples (pointing to the editorial policy) in the Organization schema
- Trust, routes: keep the Indonesian route names. Third-party checkers with English-only keyword matching may still miss /privasi, /syarat-ketentuan, or /kontak, and that is not a defect. Do not rename routes or add fake English alias links just to satisfy a checker
- Trust, freshness: show both the published date and the last updated date on every article, using the real updated_at value, and review old articles regularly
- Trust, accuracy: state official numbers from the single source of truth (the stats table and the site settings), so articles never contradict the rest of the site. Price rules apply only if a pricing module is added later. Mark general comparisons as general and ask readers to check current terms
- Helpful content: exactly one H1, a logical H1 to H2 to H3 order, scannable paragraphs, lists, and tables. The title tag and the H1 describe the same topic
- Title tag: 30 to 60 characters including the brand suffix, with the main topic first. The suffix is " | " followed by the site name from the site settings, so an article meta_title must stay at or below 60 minus the suffix length
- AI search readiness: write an answer-first lead, phrase main H2 and H3 headings as the questions people ask, and add 3 to 6 short Q&As near the end. FAQ headings count toward the H2 and H3 limits in seo-rules.md
- Allow search and AI crawlers, and allow snippets. Use data-nosnippet only on repeated site chrome such as the footer and related-article blocks
- Technical: every content image declares width and height, uses WebP or AVIF under 100 KB, and lazy-loads below the fold. Every indexable page has a self-referencing canonical, a valid viewport, lang="id", and valid JSON-LD
- Spam-policy safety: no keyword stuffing. Keep any single term well below 3% of the main content (aim for 2.5% or less), use synonyms, avoid templated filler, hidden text, and hidden links, and disclose any affiliate or sponsored link
- Human language: write like people actually talk and search on the internet, so the content matches the user's intent and still reads as written by a real person with real experience
- Verification: after changing an article or a page template, re-check the E-E-A-T score and the points above by review, and fix only what can be fixed honestly
